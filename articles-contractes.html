1 Grammaire — Grade 9

Les Articles Contractés

The Complete Guide: HOW they form, WHEN to use them, WHERE they belong

🔑 Two questions, in this order

1. Which preposition? The verb decides — à or de.
2. Does it contract? The word straight after the blank decides. Get them in the right order and the whole topic falls apart neatly.

2 The Core Concept

What happens when words crash?

A contraction is a mandatory collision. You cannot write à le, à les, de le or de les in French — they must morph into one word.

à / de + le / les = ONE new word
auà + le
auxà + les
dude + le
desde + les

💡 Rule of thumb

Preposition + Definite Article = Contraction. Take away either half and nothing happens.

3 Formula 1

Les contractions avec « À »

Used for destination (where you're going) and indirect objects (who you speak to).

💥 They collide — merge them

à+le =au Je vais au cinéma. — I'm going to the cinema.
à+les =aux Je parle aux élèves. — I'm speaking to the students.

🤝 No collision — leave them apart

à+la =à la Tu vas à la campagne.
à+l' =à l' Il est à l'hôtel.
4 Formula 2

Les contractions avec « DE »

Used for origin (where you're coming from) and possession (whose it is).

💥 They collide — merge them

de+le =du La voiture du directeur. — The director's car.
de+les =des Les cahiers des garçons. — The boys' notebooks.

🤝 No collision — leave them apart

de+la =de la Je rentre de la bibliothèque.
de+l' =de l' L'ami de l'actrice.

6 Step 1 of 2

The secret is in the verb

Don't stare at the blank. Look backwards at the verb in front of it — the verb is what decides whether you need à or de at all.

🔁 The two steps, always in this order

STEP 1 → look at the VERB → à or de ?
STEP 2 → look at the NEXT WORD → contract or not ?
The verb sends you TO somethingThe verb brings you FROM something
aller · arriver · parler · téléphoner · donner
→ use the À family		 venir · rentrer · + possession
→ use the DE family

⚠️ Why students lose marks here

They pick au or du by guessing which sounds right. The verb is not a matter of taste — venir takes de every single time.

7 Triggers for À

Which verbs demand « À » ?

Two families: verbs of movement towards a place, and verbs of communication aimed at a person.

🚶 Destination — movement TOWARDS a place

allerto go
arriverto arrive
entrerto enter

Je vais au parc. · Elle arrive à l'école.

🗣️ Communication — an action aimed AT a person

parlerto speak
téléphonerto phone
donnerto give

Je donne le livre aux élèves. · Il téléphone au médecin.

8 Triggers for DE

Which verbs demand « DE » ?

Verbs of coming away from a place — and the everyday job of saying whose something is.

🔙 Origin — movement AWAY FROM a place

venirto come
rentrerto return
sortirto go out

Il vient du stade. · Elle rentre de la bibliothèque.

🔗 Possession — the "of" that links two nouns

English uses an apostrophe; French uses de. The director's car becomes the car of the director.

C'est la voiture du directeur. · Le sac de la fille.

🧠 Flip it into English

If you can say it with "of the" or "from the", you need de. If you can say "to the", you need à.

9 Step 2 of 2

Now — does it actually contract?

The verb has given you à or de. Step 2 asks a different question: is there really a le or les for it to crash into?

🔓 The secret

A contraction can only happen if a definite article was going to be there in the first place.
If the noun doesn't need le / les — or something else has taken their place — there is nothing to contract with.

2Cities

10 No article · names

People don't take articles

In French we never say "the Paul" or "the Marie". With no le or la in front of the name, there is nothing for à or de to merge with.

✓ Write this✗ Never this
Je parle à Paul.au Paul
Paul is a name. Names carry no article, so à stays alone.
Le cahier de Marie.du Marie
La directrice parle à M. Minois.au M. Minois
Voilà le sac de Mireille.de la Mireille

💬 English gives it away

You'd say "Marie's bag", never "the Marie's bag". If the sounds wrong in English, le / la is wrong in French too.

11 No article · cities

Most cities stand alone

Cities almost never take le or la, so the preposition stays bare.

➡️ Destination — use à

Nous allons à Paris. · Il habite à Koweït.

⬅️ Origin — use de (or d' before a vowel)

Je viens de Lyon. · Il rentre d'Athènes.

⚠️ The exception: cities whose name contains the article

A few cities keep an article inside the name itself — Le Caire, Le Havre. There the article really is present, so it really does contract: Je vais au Caire.

12 Geography · countries

Countries are the opposite of cities

Cities drop the article. Countries keep it — so with countries the contraction is back on, and the form depends on the country's gender.

Going there — destinationComing from there — origin
en France · en Inde
feminine countries		 de France · d'Inde
no article at all
au Japon · au Canada
masculine — à + le		 du Japon · du Canada
de + le
aux États-Unis
plural — à + les		 des États-Unis
de + les

⚠️ « en » is not a contraction — and that is the point

en is a different preposition altogether, included here only so the set is complete. Learn it with the others or you will write au France — the single most common country mistake, made by students who were taught only au and aux.

13 Blocked · possessives

Possessives block the article

mon, ton, son, ma, ta, sa, mes, tes, ses, notre, votre, leur — these words take the place of le / la / les. The article is gone, so nothing can contract.

le → replaced by → mon
✓ Write this✗ Never this
Je donne le stylo à mon ami.au mon ami
C'est la voiture de mes parents.des mes parents
Je parle à mon père.au mon père

⚠️ Careful — des mes parents looks almost right

It is a very common slip, because des and mes both feel plural. But you only get one determiner per noun. mes is already doing the job.

14 Blocked · demonstratives

Demonstratives block it too

ce, cet, cette, ces (this / that / these) work exactly like the possessives: they stand where the article would stand, so there is no le or les left to merge.

le → replaced by → ce
✓ Write this✗ Never this
Tu vas à cet hôtel ?au cet hôtel
C'est le sac de cette fille.de la cette fille
C'est le bureau de ce professeur.du ce professeur
Nous parlons à ces filles.aux ces filles

🧠 One rule covers slides 13 and 14

Possessives and demonstratives are both determiners. A French noun takes one determiner — never two. If a determiner is already there, the article isn't.

15 Plain de · quantity

Quantity words take plain « de »

After any expression of quantity, use de (or d') on its own. Never du, never des, never de la.

beaucoupa lot
un peua little
assezenough
troptoo much
combienhow much
un kiloa kilo
✓ Write this✗ Never this
Beaucoup de devoirs.beaucoup des devoirs
Un kilo de pommes.un kilo des pommes
Un litre d'eau.un litre de l'eau
16 The CBSE trap

« du » and « des » have two different jobs

They look identical on the page. They behave completely differently — and telling them apart is where the exam marks are.

Job 1 · CONTRACTED — "of the / from the"Job 2 · PARTITIVE — "some"
Le cahier du garçon.
the boy's notebook — de + le		 Je mange du pain.
I'm eating some bread
Les jouets des enfants.
the children's toys — de + les		 J'achète des fruits.
I'm buying some fruit

⚠️ Why it matters — the negative test

Make the sentence negative and the two split apart:
Partitive collapses → Je ne mange pas de pain.
Contracted never moves → Ce n'est pas le cahier du garçon.

🧠 How to tell them apart in one second

Try replacing it with "some". If the sentence still makes sense, it's partitive. If you need "of the", it's contracted.

17 Plain de · negation

The quantity eraser: negation

The partitive articles du, de la, de l', des mean "some". Make the sentence negative and they are erased — only de or d' survives.

➕ Positive — the partitive is there

Mon frère prend de l'eau et des œufs.
My brother is having some water and some eggs.

➖ Negative — it collapses to de / d'

Il ne prend pas d'eau et il ne prend pas d'œufs.
He isn't having any water or any eggs.

⚠️ One famous exception

After être, nothing is erased: Ce n'est pas du lait. — It isn't milk. The eraser works on objects of ordinary verbs, not on descriptions after être.

🔍 Where to look

The verb already told you à or de. Now look only at the word straight after the blank — that word decides the rest.

19 Summary

The 5-second checklist

Two steps, five questions. Tap each one to see the answer.

aller, arriver, parler, téléphoner, donner → you need à.
venir, rentrer, sortir, or "of / whose" → you need de. Decide this before you look at the noun at all.

Then use plain à or de. Names and cities carry no article. à Paris · de Marie — but a country does keep its article: au Japon · en France.

Then use plain à or de. The determiner has already replaced the article. à mon ami · de cette fille

Then use de / d' on its own. beaucoup de devoirs · pas d'eau — unless the du is contracted, which never changes.

…then a real definite article is there, and you contract as normal: à + le = au, à + les = aux, de + le = du, de + les = des. And remember la and l' never contract at all.
